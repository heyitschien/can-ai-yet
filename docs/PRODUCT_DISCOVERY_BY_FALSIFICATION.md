# CanAIYet — Product Discovery by Falsification

**Status:** Strategic explanation / product-discovery doctrine.  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Authority:** This document preserves an emerging principle for how CanAIYet should discover its product through experiment. It is **not build authority**, does not authorize live runs, spend, permissions, deployment, or publication, and does not outrank the canonical build document, communication protocol, current missions, accepted evidence, or exact repository state.

---

## Executive idea

CanAIYet should not be designed from a whiteboard backward and then protected from contradictory evidence.

It should be **discovered experimentally**.

The project begins with a stable human question:

> **Can this configured AI system perform this real human job, under these conditions, and what evidence justifies delegating it?**

Everything around that question — website, leaderboard, public benchmark, enterprise assurance product, capability contract, adapter architecture, API, standards layer, workflow qualification service — remains a hypothesis until reality earns it.

The central operating principle is:

> **Do not ask experiments to confirm CanAIYet. Ask what CanAIYet must become if the experimental result is true.**

---

## 1. What the project is learning through successive experiments

The important pattern is not one result. It is the sequence of questions created by earlier results.

### Experiment 1 — Can we build a real capability exam at all?

The first question was basic:

> Can we define a business job, create a deterministic environment, let a real model act, and verify what actually happened?

That required building the course before judging the contestant:

```text
business capability
    ↓
frozen scenarios
    ↓
known starting state
    ↓
model + tools
    ↓
actions
    ↓
authoritative resulting state
    ↓
deterministic verification
```

The important discovery was not whether the model looked intelligent.

It was that **CanAIYet could manufacture an inspectable capability observation**.

That moved the project from speculation into measurement.

---

### Experiment 2 — Does changing the model change the result?

Once one model could run the course, the next question became:

> Is the observed result mostly about this particular model, or is something deeper limiting the capability?

A second frontier-model comparator produced comparative evidence against the same frozen CAP-001 suite.

This did not create a valid broad model ranking. It did something more useful: it tested one competing explanation.

If two strong models fail many of the same scenarios, then "we chose the wrong model" becomes a weaker explanation.

That creates better questions:

- Is the capability genuinely difficult?
- Are the tools inadequate?
- Is the scenario representation poor?
- Is the capability contract unfair?
- Is the workflow dependent on environment details?
- Are we measuring the wrong thing?

The model remains a variable, but **the configured system** begins to look like the more meaningful unit.

---

### Experiment 3 — Would enterprise users trust generic evidence?

The next problem appears immediately:

> Even if CanAIYet measures a model fairly in a synthetic lab, why should an enterprise use that evidence when its CRM, permissions, policies, schemas, tools, runtime, and integration behavior are different?

This is where the product begins to move beyond a benchmark website.

The question is no longer merely:

> Which model scored better?

It becomes:

> **Does capability evidence survive contact with the environment in which the work actually happens?**

That led naturally to real vendor software such as HubSpot developer/test environments: real software, real APIs, real object models, fake consequences.

The enterprise sandbox is not valuable because it is more impressive than a simulator.

It is valuable because it creates a new falsification surface.

---

### Experiment 4 — Is the business question portable?

Once a real software environment becomes available, the deeper scientific question is:

> **Can the same business exam mean the same thing after the environment changes?**

This is the portability question.

The stable object should be the business capability contract:

```text
CapabilityContractManifest
    =
portable business question
+ scenarios
+ policies
+ allowed authority
+ expected outcomes
+ forbidden outcomes
+ scoring meaning
```

The environment should explain how that question is represented locally:

```text
EnvironmentManifest
    =
object mappings
+ adapter behavior
+ permissions
+ runtime constraints
+ reset/snapshot mechanics
+ authoritative-state sources
```

If moving CAP-001 from the synthetic lab to HubSpot forces the business question itself to change, the contract may not be portable.

If the business question survives but the result changes, that change becomes evidence.

Then the job is to explain **what changed**:

- model behavior;
- representation;
- adapter mapping;
- permissions;
- API/runtime behavior;
- tool semantics;
- platform validation;
- bad synthetic assumptions;
- truly non-portable business semantics.

That is a much richer question than "can the agent use HubSpot?"

## 2. Reachability is not delegatability

Modern agents can increasingly log into applications, browse interfaces, call APIs, modify records, send messages, and complete impressive multi-step workflows.

That capability is important, but it proves only reachability and execution potential.

It does not prove safe delegation.

A strong agent may successfully handle an ordinary lead while still failing when:

- two customers share a name;
- a contact is suppressed or has asked not to be contacted;
- the conversation was already handled;
- a discount request exceeds authority;
- required information is missing;
- an appointment is unavailable;
- a permission is absent;
- an API mutation is delayed or rejected;
- the CRM represents a concept differently from the test lab;
- a tool reports success but authoritative business state does not match;
- the same scenario is repeated and produces different outcomes.

Therefore:

> **Agent reachability proves that work can be attempted. CanAIYet exists to investigate whether the work can be delegated under stated conditions.**

This distinction may become one of the durable foundations of the product.

## 3. The startup hypothesis is mutable; the measurement mission is stable

CanAIYet should preserve one stable mission while allowing the product form to change.

### Stable mission

> **Determine what work can be delegated to machines, under what conditions, with what remaining uncertainty, and what still requires human judgment.**

### Mutable product hypotheses

The project may become:

- a public capability-intelligence site;
- an independent cross-provider matrix;
- a deployment-assurance system;
- a workflow qualification service;
- a capability-contract standard;
- an enterprise testing product;
- a longitudinal capability-data/API layer;
- a higher-stakes independent assurance business;
- something not yet named.

None of these should be protected merely because they are attractive ideas.

The project should preserve the question, not a predetermined company shape.

## 4. The product should be shaped by falsification

The desired learning loop is:

```text
belief
    ↓
cheapest discriminating experiment
    ↓
evidence
    ↓
which explanations weakened?
which explanations strengthened?
    ↓
revised product / architecture / thesis
    ↓
next experiment
```

Or more compactly:

> **belief → cheapest discriminating experiment → evidence → damaged/strengthened belief → revised product → next experiment**

The important word is **damaged**.

A useful experiment must be capable of hurting the current theory.

If every result can be interpreted as confirmation, the experiment has little informational value.

CanAIYet should actively welcome results that make an earlier product idea less plausible, because they reduce the chance of spending years building around the wrong abstraction.

---

## 5. The HubSpot transfer is a product-discovery experiment

The HubSpot work should not be framed simply as:

> Can Claude/GPT operate HubSpot?

Sophisticated agents increasingly can operate software.

The more important question is:

> **Can the same portable business exam produce trustworthy, comparable evidence when moved from our laboratory into real enterprise software?**

That experiment can falsify several things at once.

It can reveal whether:

- our synthetic lab contains unrealistic assumptions;
- our capability contract is portable;
- our stable tool semantics can map faithfully into real software;
- authoritative business state can be observed deterministically;
- permissions create materially different behavior;
- environment differences dominate model identity;
- a generic benchmark result has useful external validity;
- system-level qualification is more meaningful than model ranking.

A successful transfer strengthens the idea that CanAIYet may be building a portable measurement instrument.

A failed transfer is also valuable **if the failure is attributable**.

The failure may tell us exactly which abstraction needs to change.

## 6. A stronger interpretation of the long-term asset

A static model leaderboard is relatively easy to copy.

A more interesting evidence object is:

```text
job
× scenario
× environment
× model
× provider
× tools
× permissions
× policy
× runtime
× time
→ verified outcome
```

Over time, this creates a record of capability transitions.

For example:

```text
September:
configuration X fails ambiguous identity in HubSpot

December:
new model, same contract, same environment mapping passes
```

The interesting fact is not merely that December's model scored higher.

The system has observed a **capability boundary move**.

That is longitudinal capability intelligence.

If accumulated carefully, this historical record could become more valuable than any single result because it answers not only:

> What works now?

but:

> What changed, when, under what conditions, and why?

---

## 7. The market falsifier is decision utility

Technical validity alone does not establish a startup.

Even a beautiful portable measurement instrument has weak commercial value if nobody changes a decision because of it.

The central commercial question is:

> **Will someone make a different, better, cheaper, or safer deployment decision because CanAIYet tested the capability for them?**

Examples of meaningful evidence:

- an implementation engineer chooses a different model/configuration after reviewing failure evidence;
- a business keeps human approval on a particular exception because all tested systems fail it;
- a team avoids unnecessary experimentation because a credible existing test narrows the search space;
- an organization asks CanAIYet to test its exact workflow;
- a buyer pays for deeper evidence before granting an AI system more authority;
- a customer returns when a model or environment changes and asks whether the result still holds.

Page views, curiosity, and compliments are weaker signals.

The strongest early signal is behavioral:

> **"I changed what I was going to do because of this evidence."**

The next stronger signal is economic:

> **"Can you test this for us? How much?"**

## 8. Reality may produce several different companies

CanAIYet should explicitly permit divergent outcomes.

### Outcome A — Public capability intelligence is valuable

Users repeatedly consult independent cross-provider evidence and make decisions from it.

The company becomes a capability-intelligence/data layer.

### Outcome B — Generic evidence is weak, but company-specific qualification is valuable

Organizations care primarily about their own workflow, policies, tools, and permissions.

The company becomes deployment assurance / custom workflow qualification.

### Outcome C — Test execution commoditizes, but defining the exam is scarce

Provider tooling can execute evaluations cheaply, but organizations struggle to define what a meaningful, portable, auditable business test is.

The company shifts toward capability contracts, assurance protocols, or standards.

### Outcome D — Independence matters mainly at high stakes

Low-risk customer-service workflows are easy enough that native provider testing is sufficient, while money movement, regulated operations, legal exposure, security, or high-volume customer action still demand independent evidence.

The company narrows upward toward consequential delegation.

### Outcome E — The independent layer is unnecessary

Providers or open systems solve the decision problem more credibly and cheaply, users do not alter decisions based on independent evidence, or nobody pays for the uncertainty reduction.

Then the startup thesis should be stopped or redirected.

That would be a successful falsification result, not a scientific failure.

---

## 9. A doctrine for future CanAIYet work

When a new product or architecture idea appears, do not immediately build the grand form.

Ask:

1. What belief are we currently relying on?
2. What observation would prove that belief wrong?
3. What is the cheapest serious experiment that separates the leading explanations?
4. What must remain invariant for the comparison to mean anything?
5. What evidence will be authoritative?
6. What result would cause us to continue, narrow, pivot, or stop?
7. After the result, what changed in our understanding of the product?

Every material experiment should leave behind not only a result, but an update to the map of what CanAIYet might actually be.

## 10. The product as a fossil record of discovery

There is a deeper way to understand the project.

CanAIYet is not only accumulating model results.

It is accumulating evidence about **which abstraction survives reality**.

The project history may eventually show a sequence like:

```text
benchmark idea
    ↓
real-model capability lab
    ↓
cross-model comparison
    ↓
system/configuration qualification
    ↓
synthetic → enterprise transfer
    ↓
portable capability contracts
    ↓
decision utility
    ↓
whatever real customers pull into existence next
```

In that sense, the product becomes a fossil record of disciplined discovery.

Each layer exists because an earlier experiment exposed a question that the earlier product concept could not answer.

This is not drift if the stable human question remains intact.

It is evidence-driven product formation.

---

## 11. The principle to preserve

The most important strategic statement from this stage is:

> **Do not prematurely decide what company we are building. Build an unusually trustworthy measurement instrument, expose it to increasingly real environments and real decision-makers, and watch what reality keeps asking us to become.**

And the companion rule:

> **The startup hypothesis can change. The measurement mission stays stable.**

The project should therefore continue to move through increasingly demanding contact with reality:

```text
synthetic lab
    ↓
real vendor sandbox / developer account
    ↓
customer staging
    ↓
controlled production assurance, only when justified
    ↓
real deployment decisions
    ↓
payment and repeat use
```

At every transition, the central question is not:

> Did CanAIYet look right?

It is:

> **What must CanAIYet become if this result is true?**

That question protects the project from becoming attached to its own story.
