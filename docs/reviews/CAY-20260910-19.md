## The important insights earned tonight

### Capability is not reliability

A model can demonstrate an ability once and still be inconsistent. LEAD-001 has now produced one pass and two different failures across related development heads. That is not a publishable reliability percentage, but it is strong evidence that “can do” and “can dependably do” are different questions.

### Breadth is not repeatability

Different scenarios test the width of a capability. Repeating the same frozen scenario tests variance. CanAIYet needs both eventually, but should not mix them into one statistic.

### Failure mode matters more than a single score

Wrong-person contact, ignoring a do-not-pitch signal, or mishandling an exception is qualitatively different from using an unexpected CRM stage label. The public product should preserve this distinction instead of compressing everything into a pass-rate headline.

### Benchmark fairness is now part of the product

The lab can score a frozen rule correctly while the rule itself may still be too specific for a broad public claim. Scientific integrity requires preserving the original frozen result and proposing a future v2 instead of editing v1 after seeing the model's answer.

### Evidence can be inexpensive to manufacture

The first full frontier-model suite cost about forty cents. That does not prove a business, but it establishes a useful unit-economics baseline: evidence can be cheap enough to create selectively and reuse many times.

### Demand should decide where research money goes

The project should not fill a benchmark database because it can. External search demand, internal requests, engagement, and later payment signals should determine which capability deserves the next test dollar.

### The website may be the window, not the whole asset

The possible compounding asset is the longitudinal combination of demand, capability taxonomy, frozen tests, outcomes, failure modes, repeatability, cost, provenance, and historical change.

### The business should fund uncertainty reduction

Do not pre-spend a research budget. Spend a small tranche to answer one important question, inspect what uncertainty disappeared, and only then decide whether the next tranche is justified.

> **Do not fund the database. Fund uncertainty reduction.**

## Emerging product/business thesis

CanAIYet may become a **decision-intelligence layer for AI capability** rather than merely a benchmark site.

The long-term question is:

> **What can AI safely and economically be trusted with today, under what conditions, and how is that changing?**

The deeper bet is that agentic AI makes capability probabilistic. As models begin taking real business actions, organizations need trustworthy information about where those systems can be deployed, what breaks, how severe the failures are, how repeatable success is, and what dependable completion costs.

The working strategic thesis is preserved in `docs/CAPABILITY_INTELLIGENCE_THESIS.md`.

## What would make the project more convincing

Evidence should advance in this order:

1. technical proof — achieved;
2. publication-quality/fair benchmark proof — next;
3. measurable demand — Demand Scout / direct requests;
4. stranger engagement — people actually consume and ask for more;
5. intent — emails, report/API/custom-eval interest;
6. payment — a real non-friend customer;
7. repeat/retention — recurring value.

Capital and engineering complexity should follow those gates.

## What could prove the project wrong

The project should not rationalize weak signals forever.

Serious negative evidence would include:

- little real demand for capability questions;
- relevant traffic but little interest in deeper data;
- no willingness to pay among people who make AI deployment decisions;
- human-review and refresh costs exceeding plausible revenue;
- model change making the data stale too quickly;
- providers/free platforms commoditizing the same evidence;
- benchmark scenarios proving too arbitrary to support useful public claims.

Learning this early would still be a successful use of a small validation budget.
