# CanAIYet — Explained Like I'm Five

Imagine we build a tiny pretend company inside a computer.

The pretend company has customers, emails, bills, appointments, spreadsheets, and a website. Then we give an AI a real little job, like:

> “Please answer this customer and update the customer record.”

We do **not** ask the AI afterward, “Do you think you did a good job?”

Instead, our computer referee looks at the pretend company and checks what really happened:

- Did the right customer get the message?
- Did the AI touch the right record?
- Did it accidentally email somebody it should not?
- Did it create the right appointment?
- Did it make something up?
- Did it know when to ask a person for help?

If the right things happened and none of the bad things happened, the test passes.

If the AI says, “I updated the CRM,” but the CRM did not actually change, it fails.

That is the heart of CanAIYet:

> **We give AI real jobs in a safe pretend business, then check what it actually did.**

## What is the website?

The website is the **window into the laboratory**.

A person can ask:

> “Can AI follow up with my sales leads yet?”

And instead of guessing or repeating what an AI company says, CanAIYet can show the test result, what went wrong, when it was tested, and when a person still needs to help.

## What is the database?

The database is the **report-card shelf**.

Every time we test something, we want to remember:

- which job we tested;
- which AI did it;
- which tools it had;
- what passed;
- what failed;
- how much it cost;
- how long it took;
- whether a human was still needed;
- whether the result got better or worse later.

Over time, that shelf becomes more valuable than the website itself.

## What are the searches?

The search box is also a **listening device**.

When lots of people ask:

> “Can AI do this job?”

and we have no test for it, that tells us what humans want next.

So CanAIYet learns two things at once:

1. **What AI can actually do.**
2. **What people wish AI could do.**

That second part can tell us what to test, what to implement for businesses, and maybe even what products should exist next.

## What do we have today?

We already built the pretend company, the jobs, the referee, the report format, the website, and the database structure.

We also built a simple rule-following practice robot called the **reference agent**. It proves that the laboratory works and shows us where tests catch mistakes.

But that practice robot is **not the same thing as testing a real frontier AI model**.

So the next important step is simple:

> **Put one real AI into the laboratory and give it the first job.**

Start with CAP-001: following up with a sales lead.

Then the referee checks it exactly the same way.

When we can truthfully show that result, the full idea is working end to end.

## The whole project in one little story

**The website asks the question.  
The laboratory runs the experiment.  
The referee checks what really happened.  
The database remembers the evidence.  
People's questions tell us what to test next.**

That is Can AI Yet?
