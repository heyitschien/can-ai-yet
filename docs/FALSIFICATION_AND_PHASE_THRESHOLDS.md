# CanAIYet — Falsification & Phase Thresholds

**Status:** Strategic decision framework. Not build authority. Not benchmark evidence.  
**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Purpose:** Define what must become true for CanAIYet to deserve more investment, what would weaken or kill the thesis, and when the project should transform from research into a product, business, or action layer.

## 1. The governing principle

CanAIYet should not be protected from reality by a beautiful story.

The project advances only when evidence earns the next unit of time, complexity, and money.

> **Reality earns the next dollar.**

> **Do not fund the database. Fund uncertainty reduction.**

The project should behave like a scientific program with commercial hypotheses rather than a startup story looking for confirming evidence.

---

## 2. Current competitive reality

The largest AI/platform companies are already investing heavily in agent evaluation.

As of September 2026:

- OpenAI publishes real-world capability evaluations such as GDPval and has enterprise products with built-in evaluation/optimization loops.
- Anthropic explicitly teaches lifecycle evaluation for agents and argues that agent autonomy makes evaluation more important, not less.
- Google Cloud offers agent evaluation for task completion, final responses, tool trajectories, hallucination, and safety.
- Salesforce Agentforce Testing Center provides sandboxed, scaled testing of agent actions and conversations against expected behavior.

This is simultaneously **validation and threat**.

Validation: serious providers agree that agent evaluation is becoming necessary infrastructure.

Threat: generic agent-test tooling will likely become increasingly commoditized.

CanAIYet therefore must not depend on the claim:

> "We have an eval runner."

That is unlikely to remain scarce.

The potentially scarce layer must be something stronger: independent cross-provider capability intelligence, realistic task definitions, verified outcomes, longitudinal history, demand signals, decision thresholds, and eventually permission/routing based on accumulated evidence.

---

## 3. Working probability model

These are **subjective priors**, not measured frequencies. They exist so future evidence can update them.

### A. Provider-native eval tooling becomes excellent: ~80%

This is already underway. Providers have economic incentives to make their agents easier and safer to deploy.

Expected consequence: building a generic test harness alone is not a durable moat.

### B. Generic agent-evaluation infrastructure becomes substantially commoditized: ~60–70%

Tools for scenario generation, trajectory grading, LLM-as-judge, sandboxing, regression testing, observability, and deployment gates are likely to become widely available.

Expected consequence: CanAIYet should consume these tools when useful rather than compete merely on plumbing.

### C. Provider/platform-native evals materially reduce the value of a generic independent benchmark site: ~40–50%

If CanAIYet were only "a website with scores for models," provider reports, cloud-platform eval tools, and strong open benchmarks could make that surface difficult to monetize.

### D. Big providers fully eliminate the value of an independent cross-provider capability-intelligence layer: ~20–30%

This is lower because provider-native systems have structural limits:

- each provider has an incentive to optimize and explain its own ecosystem;
- enterprises often use multiple models/providers;
- buyers may want neutral comparison rather than vendor self-evaluation;
- real workflows are more specific than general model benchmarks;
- longitudinal cross-provider history is not naturally owned by one vendor;
- procurement, risk, governance, and insurance-like decisions often value independent evidence;
- a capability layer can potentially sit above providers and route work between them.

This probability should rise quickly if providers deliver trusted, interoperable, cross-provider, task-level evidence that buyers actually use instead of independent evaluation.

### E. The entire economic thesis disappears: ~10–20%

This would require some combination of:

- agents becoming so reliable across ordinary business work that meaningful evaluation becomes trivial;
- providers making trustworthy deployment decisions automatically and cross-provider;
- generalized capability evidence proving too weak because every workflow is bespoke;
- companies refusing to pay for independent evidence even when deployment stakes are high;
- evaluation cost/review burden exceeding the value created.

The project should actively search for these conditions rather than assume they will not occur.

## 4. Why provider improvement may strengthen rather than kill CanAIYet

A common mistake is to think:

```text
models become better
        ↓
less need for evaluation
```

The opposite can happen for a long period:

```text
models become better
        ↓
people grant them more authority
        ↓
more consequential actions move to agents
        ↓
cost of unnoticed failure rises
        ↓
verification becomes more valuable
```

A weak agent writing drafts does not require the same assurance as an agent issuing refunds, changing records, executing contracts, approving claims, or operating infrastructure.

Therefore the relevant variable is not only raw model reliability.

A useful conceptual relationship is:

```text
value of verification
≈
authority delegated
× consequence of failure
× remaining uncertainty
```

As reliability rises, uncertainty may fall — but delegated authority and consequence may rise faster.

This creates a potentially long-lived measurement market.

## 5. What providers are likely to own vs. what an independent layer could own

### Providers are likely to become excellent at:

- evaluating their own models and agent stacks;
- regression testing customer agents;
- tracing tool use;
- sandbox execution;
- safety policy checks;
- performance optimization;
- provider-native deployment guidance;
- automatic test generation and monitoring.

### An independent layer could still own:

- **cross-provider neutrality** — compare systems that have different commercial incentives;
- **task-first taxonomy** — organize around real human work rather than model releases;
- **longitudinal history** — preserve when capabilities crossed meaningful thresholds;
- **demand graph** — know what humans and businesses are actually trying to delegate;
- **real-world outcome definitions** — what success/failure means in business state, not only text quality;
- **decision thresholds** — safe enough, cheap enough, supervised enough for a particular use;
- **economic comparison** — cost per successful/verified task, not just token price;
- **independent assurance** — evidence trusted by buyers, operators, auditors, procurement, consultants, and eventually software;
- **action selection** — choose a qualified system before work executes.

The durable question is therefore not:

> "Can we build better eval tooling than OpenAI, Google, Salesforce, or Anthropic?"

It is:

> **"Can we own a more neutral and useful decision layer above their tools and models?"**

## 6. Phase thresholds — when the organism earns its next form

These thresholds are **working decision gates**, not sacred KPIs. They should be revised when reality provides better baselines.

### Phase 0 — Instrument

**Question:** Can we manufacture trustworthy observations at all?

Evidence required:
- controlled environment;
- frozen scenarios;
- real model/tool execution;
- deterministic or otherwise defensible judging;
- exact provenance;
- measured cost/runtime;
- independent review before public claims.

Current status: technically demonstrated once; publication fairness still pending.

**Do not advance if:** results cannot be reproduced, provenance is weak, or human review makes each observation prohibitively expensive.

---

### Phase 1 — Publishable Evidence

**Question:** Can a skeptical outsider inspect the evidence and agree the claim is fair?

Working threshold:
- at least one independently reviewed capability report that can be publicly defended;
- no hidden mismatch between headline and scenario evidence;
- clear limitations and non-statistical claims where sample size is small;
- cost of producing/updating the report is measured.

**Tipping point:** CanAIYet stops being merely an internal experiment and becomes a credible public research instrument.

---

### Phase 2 — Demand

**Question:** Are people independently asking questions we are equipped to test?

Working threshold examples:
- several capability families emerge from external search/request data rather than founder invention;
- direct conversations with relevant operators repeatedly surface the same uncertainty;
- internal searches/requests begin producing non-random clusters;
- at least a few requests arise without us first proposing the exact capability.

A practical early bar might be **3+ independently recurring capability families across multiple demand sources**, not one viral query.

**Do not advance if:** demand appears only after prompting people with our own examples.

---

### Phase 3 — Decision Utility

**Question:** Does the evidence actually change behavior?

Working threshold examples:
- at least ~5 relevant users can point to a concrete deployment/model/workflow decision influenced by the evidence;
- at least 2 organizations ask for a deeper/custom test without being pushed into it;
- users inspect failure modes/methodology rather than only consuming a headline score.

**Tipping point:** We are no longer selling curiosity. We are reducing decision uncertainty.

---

### Phase 4 — Payment

**Question:** Will a real buyer pay to reduce that uncertainty?

Working threshold:
- at least 3 non-friend, non-subsidized paid evaluations or equivalent purchases;
- model/infrastructure cost and human labor are measured;
- contribution margin is positive or shows a credible path to positive;
- buyers value the decision outcome, not merely the novelty of AI benchmarking.

**Tipping point:** CanAIYet becomes a business rather than a research project.

---

### Phase 5 — Repeat / Refresh Value

**Question:** Does capability intelligence need to be refreshed often enough that customers return?

Working threshold examples:
- repeat custom-eval customers;
- retest/monitoring requests;
- customers ask "has this changed since the last model release?";
- at least some users pay repeatedly rather than once.

**Tipping point:** The longitudinal dataset begins to have economic value, not only archival value.

---

### Phase 6 — Data Advantage

**Question:** Is accumulated history becoming more useful than a fresh one-off test?

Signals:
- customers explicitly use trend/history in decisions;
- historical failure modes predict where new releases should be tested;
- the demand graph materially improves research prioritization;
- old frozen tasks allow credible capability-transition analysis;
- a new entrant could reproduce today's test but not the useful historical context.

**Tipping point:** The corpus begins to compound.

---

### Phase 7 — Decision / Action Layer

**Question:** Can software safely use our evidence before execution?

Possible interface:

```text
job + risk tolerance + budget
        ↓
capability evidence
        ↓
qualified agent/model/configuration
        ↓
authorize / supervise / escalate / refuse
        ↓
execution
        ↓
outcome verification
```

Requirements before this phase:
- substantial accepted evidence coverage;
- repeated observations where reliability claims are made;
- decision thresholds grounded in real customer needs;
- clear uncertainty handling;
- monitoring/retest cadence;
- external demand for machine-readable recommendations;
- strong separation between "tested" and "safe for this customer's exact environment."

**Tipping point:** CanAIYet moves from information people read to infrastructure that helps determine what happens next.

Do not build this merely because it is exciting. It must be pulled into existence by demand and accumulated evidence.

## 7. The most important unknown unknowns to watch

### 1. Workflow specificity may dominate model identity

We may discover that the important object is not "Claude can do lead follow-up" but:

> "This exact agent + instructions + tools + permissions + CRM schema + escalation policy can do this workflow."

If so, CanAIYet shifts from model benchmarking toward **system/configuration qualification**.

That may make custom evaluation more valuable while weakening generic public rankings.

### 2. The true moat may be scenario quality, not result volume

Millions of cheap runs are not automatically valuable.

A smaller set of realistic, carefully defined tasks with strong judges may be worth more than a giant noisy corpus.

The scarce intellectual asset could be knowing what constitutes a meaningful exam.

### 3. Independence may matter only at higher stakes

For low-risk workflows, customers may accept provider-native testing.

Independent evidence may become valuable primarily when stakes rise: money movement, legal exposure, customer harm, regulated processes, high-volume operations, security, or procurement.

If this pattern appears, CanAIYet should climb toward high-value decisions rather than chase mass low-stakes coverage.

### 4. Standards may become more valuable than scores

If many organizations need agent assurance, the strongest asset may eventually be a common capability taxonomy, test protocol, or qualification standard rather than a proprietary leaderboard.

### 5. Verification may move inside execution

The final system may not only decide which agent is qualified. It may verify the result after every consequential action and dynamically change routing when behavior drifts.

That would turn capability intelligence into a closed loop:

```text
select → act → verify → update evidence → select again
```

This is a much deeper system than today's website, and should only be pursued if earlier phases earn it.

## 8. Competitive falsification radar

Track these quarterly or when major platform releases occur:

1. Can OpenAI/Anthropic/Google/etc. evaluate **cross-provider** agents, not only their own stack?
2. Do they publish **real workflow-level reliability** rather than broad benchmarks?
3. Can a buyer bring a workflow and receive an automatic, trusted deployment verdict?
4. Are results accepted by procurement/risk teams without independent review?
5. Do platforms provide longitudinal history across providers/configurations?
6. Do customers still pay third parties for independent evaluations?
7. Are open-source evaluation suites becoming good enough that scenario/judge creation is trivial?
8. Does agent reliability become high enough that ordinary workflows no longer justify measurement?
9. Does regulation/insurance/procurement create more demand for independent assurance instead?
10. Does provider concentration reduce or increase demand for a neutral layer?

If answers 1–5 become strongly "yes" and 6 becomes strongly "no," CanAIYet's independent intelligence thesis weakens materially.

## 9. Stop / narrow / pivot logic

### Continue / invest more when:
- each new tranche removes a meaningful uncertainty;
- external demand is rising;
- evidence changes decisions;
- buyers request/pay for deeper evaluation;
- longitudinal history begins to matter;
- provider-native tools help us lower costs without replacing our decision value.

### Narrow when:
- only a few capability domains show strong demand;
- independent evaluation matters mainly in regulated/high-stakes work;
- custom evaluations work but broad public coverage does not.

### Pivot when:
- generic benchmark pages have weak value but workflow qualification is valuable;
- provider tooling commoditizes test execution but customers still need independent scenario design, audit, or decision policy;
- the valuable object becomes configuration certification rather than model ranking.

### Stop when:
- no meaningful demand appears after fair testing;
- target users do not change decisions from the evidence;
- nobody pays for independent assurance;
- review/refresh cost cannot plausibly support revenue;
- provider-native or open systems solve the same decision problem more cheaply and more credibly;
- longitudinal data does not improve decisions enough to matter.

Stopping after those signals would be a successful experiment, not failure.

---

## 10. The plant-near-sunlight principle

Do not force CanAIYet to become the imagined 2031 platform.

Place the organism near real signals and observe where it grows:

```text
search demand
custom requests
engagement
changed decisions
payment
repeat usage
provider competition
regulation
agent capability changes
```

> **Put the plant near sunlight and watch which direction it bends.**

Our job is not to dictate its final shape. Our job is to keep the experiment honest enough that the direction has informational value.

---

## One-sentence operating thesis

**CanAIYet should advance from laboratory → public evidence → demand → decision utility → payment → repeat intelligence → data advantage → action layer only when reality earns each transition, while continuously testing whether provider-native evaluation is validating the market or eliminating the need for an independent layer.**
